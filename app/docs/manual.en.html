C3D Assistant turns a PDF plan into a 3D pipe network for Civil 3D (water, sewer, storm drain, gas, electrical and telecom). Everything is measured in feet (and diameters in inches).

The whole job, in 5 steps

1 Open the PDF plan (or start with a blank canvas).
2 Draw the pipes on top of the plan, click by click.
3 Fill in their data: type, size and elevations (depth).
4 Check the colored signs that appear on the plan.
5 Export to DXF and open it in Civil 3D with the import button.

How to use this manual

  • Pick a chapter in the list on the left, or turn pages with Previous and Next.
  • Text too small? Use the  A+  and  A−  buttons below. The app remembers the size.
  • Green boxes are tips; yellow boxes are things to pay attention to.
Tip: don't be afraid to try things.  Ctrl+Z  undoes the last change and  Ctrl+Shift+Z  redoes it.

Option A — Open a PDF plan

  1. Menu File → Open PDF… (or drag the PDF onto the window).
  2. If the PDF was plotted from CAD, an assistant helps you prepare the sheet (see the chapter «CAD PDF plans»).
  3. If it is a scan, you first compose the sheet (crop, straighten and join its sheets) and then draw on it (see the chapter «CAD PDF plans», scans section).

Option B — Blank canvas (no PDF)

  1. Menu File → New canvas… or  Ctrl+N .
  2. Choose the sheet size (A4, Letter, Tabloid, ANSI, ARCH… or custom sizes in millimeters or inches) and the orientation.
  3. Choose the plan scale, for example 1" = 20'. The window shows how much real ground fits on the sheet.
  4. Press Create. You draw and export just like on a PDF.

Saving and opening your work

  • Save:  Ctrl+S . A .digproj file with the whole project is created.
  • Save with another name:  Ctrl+Shift+S .
  • Open a project: File → Open project…
Attention: a dot ● next to the title means there are unsaved changes. Save often.

When the PDF comes straight from CAD, the app can read the plan's lines and save you from drawing them. When you open it, the app checks whether it is a CAD plan or a scan; if it is not sure, it asks you. A scan (or a traced plan without text or layers) goes through «Compose sheet» and then to manual drawing (see «Image or scanned PDF» below).

The assistant has 3 steps. At the top of each screen you will see 1 Compose sheet › 2 Sheet layers › 3 Preview: the current step is highlighted and earlier steps can be clicked to go back without losing your work. At the bottom, across the whole width, are the view options (such as Opacity) and the Cancel and Continue buttons.

When a step takes a few seconds (large sheets, several pieces, many utilities), the window or the panel being updated is dimmed and a «Loading…» card appears with a spinning indicator and what the app is doing (for example «Recognizing… · Storm Drain (3 of 6)»). There is no need to click anything: the card goes away by itself when it finishes.

1. Compose work sheet

This step has three tabs, each one full screen: Source, Area to take and Composite sheet. You can switch between them at any time by clicking them, with ‹ Back / Next › at the bottom, or with Ctrl+PgUp / Ctrl+PgDn. The Composite sheet tab shows how many pieces you have and Area to take shows a ! if you left something half done.

  1. Source: choose the PDF (Add PDF… adds others) and the sheet in the thumbnail gallery. A sheet marked «no layers» is a flattened plan: you can use it, but recognition will not find utilities on it. Sheets marked «✔ Taken» are already on the composite sheet. Double-click a sheet, or Take area of this sheet, to go to the next tab.
  2. Area to take: the top says which sheet you are viewing (‹ Sheet 21 of 50 ›); the arrows move to the previous or next sheet without going back to Source. Draw a rectangle around the part of the plan you need and press Take area (or Full sheet); a green confirmation appears with See composite sheet ›. The sheet scale is next to those buttons; snapping to lines and trimming the border line are under Options. Picking a sheet does not add it: until you take it, a notice reminds you.
  3. Composite sheet: drag each piece into place. With the Magnet on, the lines of neighboring pieces snap together; with Bridges, gaps between sheets are filled in (both are under Joins).
  4. Click a piece to rotate it, remove it, fix its fine angle or its scale under Adjust, or adjust its crop with Edit area.
  5. At the bottom left you see what is missing to go on. With no piece, Continue is disabled. If you marked an area and did not take it, or you are viewing a sheet you did not take, a notice says so (with a link to fix it) and on continuing the app asks what to do: take it, use only that sheet, go on without it or go back.
  6. Continue goes to step 2.

Image or scanned PDF: composing by hand

A scan also goes through Compose work sheet (or menu Tools → Compose image/scanned PDF…), but without layers or recognition: at the end, Import sheet into editor leaves it as the background to draw by hand.

  • In Area to take you drag a rectangle and then move each corner separately to follow a skewed edge of the scan.
  • In Composite sheet all tools are in a single bar: guides on the left (always in the same place) and the selected piece's actions on the right (rotate 90°, Adjust, remove). If the panel is narrow, buttons show only their icon; the name appears on hover. Below, a line tells you what to do with the active tool.
  • Ruler ▾: one click shows or hides it; the ▾ arrow opens its options (Lock ruler, Rotate 90°, Bring into view, length, width, position and angle). Its bottom edge is the alignment guide; the green dot at its end rotates it and snaps to 0° and 90°. When locked (red padlock) it does not move and sheets are dragged underneath.
  • Protractor: the degrees of the selected piece, always the same size on screen. Drag the green dot to rotate it about its centre: it snaps to 0°, 90°, 180° and 270° (the reading turns green with ✔). Ctrl = fine adjustment (the mouse turns 10 times less, in hundredths), Shift = 15° steps, double-click on the dot = back to 0°.
  • With a piece selected: arrows move it (Ctrl 0.1 pt, Shift 10 pt) and + / − rotate it 0.1° (Ctrl 0.01°, Shift 1°). Adjust holds the exact angle, the scale and Rotate to 0°.
  • Measure: click two points; the line follows the mouse with the distance in feet and the bearing (Shift = horizontal/vertical). If both points fall on the same piece, Calibrate scale… appears: type the real length (for example between two stations 100 ft apart) and that sheet's scale is corrected.
  • Straighten: click two points on a line that should be horizontal or vertical and the piece rotates to straighten it (it picks the nearest axis); the line stays in place. Esc cancels the marked point or leaves the tool.
  • Blend edges (on, in green): where two pieces overlap the ink of both shows and one piece's paper does not hide the other's lines. Overlap the pieces slightly at the join and no cut or white strip is left; it also applies to the sheet sent to the editor. Each piece's outline only shows when selected or under the mouse.

The ruler and the protractor are aids: they do not appear on the editor sheet or in the DXF.

2. Sheet layers

  • Recognize: check which utilities the app should read (Electrical, Storm drain, Water, Sewer, Gas and Telecom). They all come checked; All checks or unchecks them at once. Only the ones the sheet has can be checked, and at least one is needed to continue.
  • Plan layers: layers are grouped by utility (Water, Sewer, Storm drain, Gas, Electrical, Telecom, Other), each with its color. The utility's checkbox turns all its layers on or off; the ▸ arrow expands it so you can choose layer by layer. What you uncheck is hidden from the plan and not used for recognition.
  • By the letters on the line: the app does not only look at the layer name; it also reads the letters each line carries (—TE—, —SC—, —W—, —G—, —ss—…). A layer whose name is not a utility's, but whose lines say so, appears under its utility with the letters next to it, for example U-TRPW-DBNK-P (517) «TE» under Electrical (hover to see why), and it is recognized. The letters follow the plan's legend: «TE» and «SE» are electrification duct banks (Electrical), «SC» signal & communication (Telecom). In layers that mix everything (_Xref, G-XREF, layer 0) each line goes to the utility of its letters and the rest is left alone. Overhead «(oh)» and unknown «unk» lines are not recognized.
  • «Legend» panel (on the left): at the top, By the letters on their line lists those layers as cards: the utility and its letters, the layer, why (its name does not say the utility, or says another one) and what the PDF's legend says about those letters. Each card's checkbox decides whether they are used: unchecked, the layer goes back to its group by name and is not recognized by its letters (the decision is saved in the project). Below, Plan legend shows the legend that comes in the PDF itself (each line's sample and its description), the lines on this sheet first; See all shows the rest. Clicking a card or a row highlights those lines on the sheet and zooms in; another click shows everything again. If the PDF has no legend and there are no layers by letters, the panel collapses by itself (‹ / › collapses and expands it).
  • Type in Search layer… to find a layer by name; the eye buttons show or hide all the layers visible in the list.
  • Opacity lightens the plan (and switches the background to white or black), just like in the editor.
  • If the sheet has several pages, ◀ Sheet N / M ▶ changes sheet without leaving.
  • To go back to step 1, click 1 Compose sheet in the bar at the top; Continue recognizes the sheet and opens step 3. If you cancel here, the sheet is loaded in the editor without recognition, so you can draw by hand.

3. Recognition preview

  • Shows the lines found for each utility, each with its color, and their vaults or manholes. The screen has three columns: To check on the left, the sheet in the middle and the summary on the right.
  • On the right: the sheet and its scale, cards with the totals (segments, abandoned, bends, structures), a bar per utility (active solid, abandoned hatched) and the Coverage: how much of the plan's strokes was covered (green ✔ = complete). If the quality check marked something on the sheet, it says what below: uncovered dashes (magenta line), vaults without a line (magenta ring) and off-pattern strokes (dotted turquoise: arrows, leaders). Those colors belong to no utility.
  • At the bottom, Layers used: the layers taken as lines and vaults. Click one (or pick it with the arrow keys and press Enter) and the sheet shows it: that layer's items get a halo in their color, the rest fades and the view zooms to that area; below the title it says how many lines and vaults there are. Clicking Lines, Vaults or the utility name shows that whole group. Another click on the same row, or ✕ Show all, shows the whole sheet again.
  • On the left, To check: these are not errors. The sheet was already recognized; these are points worth a look before importing (a curve imported as an approximation, a vault no line reaches…). Each one says below what was found and what was done with it. Only what shows in red is a real problem (for example, a sheet without layers). The points are grouped by utility. Click one (or reach it with Tab and press Enter): the view takes you to the exact spot on the sheet, marked with a black and white box. Each click shows the next case (1/N). Once you have seen all its cases it is marked ✔ and every visited spot on the sheet keeps a dashed green box; at the top you see your progress («1 of 4 seen»). One with no spot is marked with one click; right-click sets it back to pending. Extra information is folded under Details. The ‹ button collapses the panel to see the sheet larger; collapsed, a strip shows «To check (N)».
  • At the bottom left: Opacity lightens the plan so you can better see what and how much was recognized, and Join segments (green = on) joins segments that continue straight ahead into one pipe.
  • To go back, click 2 Sheet layers or 1 Compose sheet in the bar at the top. Adjust layers… is only needed if the plan uses other layer names: there you say which are lines and which are vaults (also if you disagree with a line that was recognized by its letters: what you choose there rules).
  • Continue and import into the editor adds them to the project as pipes; Cancel leaves the editor empty.
  • Abandoned lines are imported marked (AB): those on the «-A» layer with «/» marks, and any line with double «//» marks.
  • Curved bends on the plan are imported with their real radius, including very open curves, small rounded corners, a curve that leaves another line or ends loose and two curves joined with no straight segment between them (an «S» curve: one bend per curve, touching at the inflection point). Each bend covers its whole curve on the plan, end to end. A dashed bend is approximate (on the plan it is not an exact arc): check it.
  • Each line ends where its ink ends: if on the plan it stops in mid-air it is not joined to the neighboring line. If it stops at its letter (or at a gap) right before the edge of the view, it reaches the plan's cut; on a composite sheet, lines that cross the seam still run from one piece to the other. A line whose letter is covered by a text label on the plan stays continuous.
  • Magnet to vaults and manholes: a line that reaches a vault or manhole and was left less than 3 pt from its edge is brought to the drawn edge, along its own line (no kinks); a loose line, only if it is less than 1.5 pt away. On import, the box, solid or manhole is joined to that line instead of left loose at the center. A vault with no line nearby is not touched. The Line ends joined to their vault notice takes you to each one.
  • If a pipe is drawn with its two walls (two parallel lines running together from start to end) a single utility is imported, along the middle line. The same if it has walls and a centerline (the centerline is used), walls and a colored filled body, dashed walls or a thin closed outline (its centerline is used). It applies to every utility. The notice pipes with two walls takes you to each one. Three or more parallel lines together are left untouched.
  • If the plan has the same line drawn twice on the same layer (overlapping, with shifted dashes), a single line is imported.
  • Water and gas come in without boxes (they are pressure networks). Sewer and storm drain come in with their manholes; electrical and telecom, with a box only where the plan has a vault.

Composing again later

Menu Tools → Compose work sheet… opens step 1 again, on the PDF and sheet you were working on, and repeats steps 2 and 3 with the new sheet. Everything is saved in the project (.digproj), including the source PDFs.

Tip: if something is not recognized, you can always draw it by hand (next chapter).
  1. Open the Draw utility section of the left panel.
  2. Choose the type: water, sewer, storm drain, gas, electrical or telecom.
  3. Click on the plan at each point the pipe passes through.
  4. Press  Enter  to finish.

The green magnet (to join pipes precisely)

When you get close to another pipe of the same type, a green mark appears. Its shape tells you what the click will snap to:

Circle: the end of another pipe.
Square: a vertex (a bend) of another pipe.
Triangle: a point in the middle of a segment.

If you start at the end of another pipe of the same type, the app asks whether to continue it (same utility) or create a new one that touches it.

Tip: to fix a pipe, select it in the list and use Edit/move (right click), or Precise move to move it by an exact distance.

Select the pipe in the Utilities list. In the Properties panel:

Extended data

The green View extended data button (next to «Delete», also in Manholes) shows a Field | Value table:

  • If the pipe or vault came from the PDF, it carries locked 🔒 data: the layer it came from and what its name says (type, whether it is new, existing or abandoned, and the source sheet). They are for reference only.
  • With Add field you write down your own data. It is saved in the project and goes into the DXF.
Important about the name: the name only identifies the network. Two pipes of the same type that touch are joined in Civil 3D even if their names differ: they go into the network of the first name.

Curves

In the Curves list you can turn a bend into a curve and set its radius (in feet). If the number turns red, the radius does not fit the neighboring segments: the app shows the maximum allowed.

  • The app automatically places a manhole (BZ-) at each sewer and storm drain vertex.
  • Electrical and telecom networks have few boxes: the app only places a box (CAJA-) where the PDF plan has a real vault, with its measurements. You add the rest.
  • In the Manholes list you change their family, size and elevations.
  • Square boxes coming from the PDF show as SÓLIDO (solid): no family; you adjust their Length and Width (the drawing resizes) and their Height (2 m by default). In Civil 3D they are created as 3D solids.
  • To add one in the middle of a line: Tools → Insert manhole in-line…
  • Hide manhole removes a manhole that does not really exist; the network stays connected.
Water and gas are pressure networks: they have no manholes. Where pipes meet, Civil 3D places a fitting (elbow, Tee, Wye or cross).

A duct bank is a concrete block with several conduits inside.

  1. Right-click an electrical or telecom pipe → Create duct bank for this pipe (or the Open Duct Bank Designer button).
  2. Draw the section: block size, margins and rounded corners.
  3. Add the conduits. The outline is green if it fits and red if it breaks a rule (minimum spacing or edge clearance).
  4. Save and close. Civil 3D creates the 3D block with its conduits.

One design for several pipes. In Assign to: check every pipe that carries the same duct bank (the filter and the All / None buttons help). Hover a pipe in the list to see its thumbnail on the plan before checking it. You can also select several in the Utilities list ( Ctrl +click,  Shift +click or  Ctrl+A ) and right-click → Create one duct bank for the N utilities or Assign existing duct bank. In the Duct banks list, hover a row to see the section.

Tip: the Show dimensions button draws the distances between conduits.  Esc  drops the active tool without closing the window.

While you draw, the app checks the design and places signs where something needs your attention. Hover over a sign to read what is going on, or click it for the details. They only appear at crossings and joints of pressure network pipes (water and gas). On storm drain, sewer, electrical and telecom you are only warned when two pipes cross at the same elevation without being connected («Clashing pipes»).

The red triangle appears in these cases. All refer to pressure networks (water and gas), except «clashing pipes», which warns on any utility.

CaseWhen it appearsWhat Civil 3D will do
Too many pipes 5 or more pipes meet at the same point at the same elevation. It will not place any fitting there. Fix the drawing.
Different networks Two pressure networks of a different type (water and gas) cross at the same elevation. It does not join them: they will clash. Change one elevation.
Clashing pipes Two pipes of any utility (at least one that is not water or gas) cross each other at the same elevation and are not connected: for example water and storm drain, or two electrical lines crossing mid-segment without a box. It does not join them: they will clash. Fix the drawing or change one elevation.
Elevation step At a vertex of one pipe, the incoming and outgoing segments have different elevations. It joins both segments at the average elevation.
Segment too short between elbows Between two consecutive bends there is a segment shorter than 3 diameters (minimum 1 foot). It places a single elbow instead of two.
Sloped vertical connection An approved vertical joint at a bend has too little elevation difference for two elbows. It places a Wye with a sloped branch and the incoming pipe gets a slope.
No room for a vertical Two pipes end at the same point at different heights, but the difference is too small to join them with a vertical pipe. It gives a slope to the longer one (or to both, if they are the same length) and joins them with an elbow.
Return bend The pipe turns back almost in a U (turn over 135°) and the short segment is not long enough for the curve. It places a U curve at the vertex and shifts the short segment slightly to the side.
These alerts do not appear on storm drain and sewer: there the manhole handles drops and joints.

Places the plan at its real location (Los Angeles coordinates, EPSG:2229). It is for preliminary design: exact data comes from the survey.

  1. Tools → Georeference…
  2. On the map on the right, type an address or a street intersection and press Search and download.
  3. Click a point on the plan (left) and then the same point on the map. Clicks snap to intersections and corners.
  4. Repeat with 3 or more points, preferably far apart.
  5. Press Fit + RMSE. RMSE is the average error in feet: the lower, the better.
  6. Press Save georeference.

 Ctrl+Z  deletes the last point. If you know the Civil 3D coordinate system code (for example CA83VF), type it and the drawing will be set up with it.

In the app

  1. Check that there are no red signs you don't understand.
  2. On the top bar, choose your Civil 3D version (2025, 2026 or 2027) and its language.
  3. Press Export DXF and choose where to save it.

In Civil 3D

  1. Open the plugin panel (command PANEL_REDES).
  2. Press Import network from DXF and choose the file.
  3. The networks are created with their pipes, manholes, curves and duct banks.

What you will see in 3D

  • On water and gas, joints get solid fittings: elbow (bend), Tee (90° branch), Wye (angled branch) and cross (4 pipes). Pipes go into the fitting's bell.
  • Each fitting stores its data (type, angle, diameters, material, network) in its properties.
  • Approved vertical joints are drawn with elbows and a vertical segment.
Attention: if you use your own families, install them first with Tools → Install custom family… and then run PREPARAR_FAMILIAS in Civil 3D.

To add extra information to the pipes in Civil 3D (installation date, specification…):

  1. In the plugin panel: Export pipes to Excel. A sheet with each pipe's name and type is created.
  2. In Excel, add one column per piece of data and fill it in. Leave blank what doesn't apply.
  3. In the panel: Import Property Sets from Excel.
  4. Check it in Civil 3D: select a pipe → Properties → Extended Data.

You can import the same file several times: nothing gets duplicated.

Fitting data (elbows, tees, wyes, crosses)

Every fitting the plugin creates carries its data automatically. Select it → Properties → Extended Data → PDFCAD_Accesorio:

  • Tipo_Accesorio: fitting type (Codo = elbow, Tee, Wye, Cruz = cross).
  • Angulo_Grados: the fitting angle, in degrees.
  • Diametro_Pulg: the diameter in inches («12 x 8» if one outlet is smaller).
  • Material: the pipe material.
  • Cota_Eje_Pies: the elevation of the fitting's center, in feet.

A drawing imported with an older version? Type PS_ACCESORIOS in Civil 3D and its fittings get this data.

I don't see my pipes in Civil 3D

Check that you chose the same Civil 3D version and language on the top bar, and that the pipes have elevations.

A yellow sign appears where two pipes join

That is normal: hover over it to see which fitting Civil 3D will place. If it says they clash, fix the elevation.

I renamed a pipe and now it is in another network

Pipes of the same type that touch always go into the same network; it takes the first name.

I made a mistake

 Ctrl+Z  undoes it. If you closed without saving, open the last saved .digproj.
