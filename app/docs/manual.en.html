C3D Assistant turns a PDF plan into a 3D pipe network for Civil 3D (water, sewer, storm drain, gas, electrical and telecom). Everything is measured in feet (and diameters in inches).

The whole job, in 5 steps

1 Open the PDF plan (or start with a blank canvas).
2 Draw the pipes on top of the plan, click by click.
3 Fill in their data: type, size and elevations (depth).
4 Check the colored signs that appear on the plan.
5 Export to DXF and open it in Civil 3D with the import button.

How to use this manual

  • Pick a chapter in the list on the left, or turn pages with Previous and Next.
  • Text too small? Use the  A+  and  A−  buttons below. The app remembers the size.
  • Green boxes are tips; yellow boxes are things to pay attention to.
Tip: don't be afraid to try things.  Ctrl+Z  undoes the last change and  Ctrl+Shift+Z  redoes it.

Option A — Open a PDF plan

  1. Menu File → Open PDF… (or drag the PDF onto the window).
  2. If the PDF was plotted from CAD, an assistant helps you prepare the sheet (see the chapter «CAD PDF plans»).
  3. If it is a scan, you first compose the sheet (crop, straighten and join its sheets) and then draw on it (see the chapter «CAD PDF plans», scans section).

Option B — Blank canvas (no PDF)

  1. Menu File → New canvas… or  Ctrl+N .
  2. Choose the sheet size (A4, Letter, Tabloid, ANSI, ARCH… or custom sizes in millimeters or inches) and the orientation.
  3. Choose the plan scale, for example 1" = 20'. The window shows how much real ground fits on the sheet.
  4. Press Create. You draw and export just like on a PDF.

Saving and opening your work

  • Save:  Ctrl+S . A .digproj file with the whole project is created.
  • Save with another name:  Ctrl+Shift+S .
  • Open a project: File → Open project…
Attention: a dot ● next to the title means there are unsaved changes. Save often.

Hiding the side panels

Both panels (Tools on the left and Inventory on the right) can hide themselves, just like Civil 3D palettes:

  1. Press the pin 📌 on the panel header (or View → Auto-hide the left / right panel).
  2. The panel becomes a vertical tab on its edge and the plan gets that space.
  3. Hover over the tab (or click it): the panel opens over the plan. When you move the mouse away or click on the plan, it folds back.
  4. To keep it open again, press the pin on its header.
Tip: while you type in a field of the panel, or a drop-down or a window is open, the panel does not fold by itself. Drag its inner edge to make it wider. The app remembers how you left each panel.

The panels adapt to their width: long buttons wrap onto two lines and button rows rearrange, so you never need to scroll sideways.

When the PDF comes straight from CAD, the app can read the plan's lines and save you from drawing them. When you open it, the app checks whether it is a CAD plan or a scan; if it is not sure, it asks you. A scan (or a traced plan without text or layers) goes through «Compose sheet» and then to manual drawing (see «Image or scanned PDF» below).

The assistant has 3 steps. At the top of each screen you will see 1 Compose sheet › 2 Sheet layers › 3 Preview: the current step is highlighted and earlier steps can be clicked to go back without losing your work. At the bottom, across the whole width, are the view options (such as Opacity) and the Cancel and Continue buttons. The Esc key does not close these windows (it only clears a highlight): to leave, use Cancel.

When a step takes a few seconds (large sheets, several pieces, many utilities), the window or the panel being updated is dimmed and a «Loading…» card appears with a spinning indicator and what the app is doing (for example «Recognizing… · Storm Drain (3 of 6)»). There is no need to click anything: the card goes away by itself when it finishes.

1. Compose work sheet

This step has three tabs, each one full screen: Source, Area to take and Composite sheet. You can switch between them at any time by clicking them, with ‹ Back / Next › at the bottom, or with Ctrl+PgUp / Ctrl+PgDn. The Composite sheet tab shows how many pieces you have and Area to take shows a ! if you left something half done.

  1. Source: choose the PDF (Add PDF… adds others) and the sheet in the thumbnail gallery. The sheet you choose is the one used: on opening, the sheet in view is already on the composite sheet and a click on another one switches it; the bottom says «Ready to continue: full sheet N». Its thumbnail says «✔ Full sheet» or «✔ Area taken». A sheet marked «no layers» is a flattened plan: you can use it, but recognition will not find utilities on it. Double-click a sheet, or Take area of this sheet, to go to the next tab.
  2. Area to take: the top says which sheet you are viewing (‹ Sheet 21 of 50 ›); the arrows move to the previous or next sheet without going back to Source (they also choose it). The whole sheet is shown with its border and handles. If you only need part of it, drag a rectangle over the plan or move the corners: the composite sheet updates by itself, with no button to press; Full sheet uses the whole sheet again. The sheet scale is at the bottom right; snapping to lines and trimming the border line are under Options.
  3. Several sheets: with the first one's area already marked, go to another sheet: a notice says it is not in yet; mark its area and press Take area (or Full sheet). In Source, Ctrl+click on a thumbnail adds it whole. If you actually wanted another sheet instead of the first one, the notice offers Use only this sheet.
  4. Composite sheet: drag each piece into place. With the Magnet on, the lines of neighboring pieces snap together; with Bridges, gaps between sheets are filled in (both are under Joins).
  5. Click a piece to rotate it, remove it, fix its fine angle or its scale under Adjust, or adjust its crop with Edit area.
  6. At the bottom left you see what is missing to go on. If you removed every piece, Continue is disabled and the Use sheet N link puts it back. If you marked an area for another piece and did not take it, or you are viewing a sheet you did not take, a notice says so (with a link to fix it) and on continuing the app asks what to do: take it, use only that sheet, go on without it or go back.
  7. You can join many sheets. If the composite sheet is very large (more than about 6 sheets of 36"×24"), the editor shows it at a lower resolution and, when you zoom in, the part you see is redrawn sharp; everything else works the same. Building it and reading its layers takes a few seconds per sheet.
  8. Continue goes to step 2.

Image or scanned PDF: composing by hand

A scan also goes through Compose work sheet (or menu Tools → Compose image/scanned PDF…), but without layers or recognition: at the end, Import sheet into editor leaves it as the background to draw by hand.

  • In Area to take you drag a rectangle and then move each corner separately to follow a skewed edge of the scan.
  • In Composite sheet all tools are in a single bar: guides on the left (always in the same place) and the selected piece's actions on the right (rotate 90°, Adjust, remove). If the panel is narrow, buttons show only their icon; the name appears on hover. Below, a line tells you what to do with the active tool.
  • Ruler ▾: one click shows or hides it; the ▾ arrow opens its options (Lock ruler, Rotate 90°, Bring into view, length, width, position and angle). Its bottom edge is the alignment guide; the green dot at its end rotates it and snaps to 0° and 90°. When locked (red padlock) it does not move and sheets are dragged underneath.
  • Protractor: the degrees of the selected piece, always the same size on screen. Drag the green dot to rotate it about its centre: it snaps to 0°, 90°, 180° and 270° (the reading turns green with ✔). Ctrl = fine adjustment (the mouse turns 10 times less, in hundredths), Shift = 15° steps, double-click on the dot = back to 0°.
  • With a piece selected: arrows move it (Ctrl 0.1 pt, Shift 10 pt) and + / − rotate it 0.1° (Ctrl 0.01°, Shift 1°). Adjust holds the exact angle, the scale and Rotate to 0°.
  • Measure: click two points; the line follows the mouse with the distance in feet and the bearing (Shift = horizontal/vertical). If both points fall on the same piece, Calibrate scale… appears: type the real length (for example between two stations 100 ft apart) and that sheet's scale is corrected.
  • Straighten: click two points on a line that should be horizontal or vertical and the piece rotates to straighten it (it picks the nearest axis); the line stays in place. Esc cancels the marked point or leaves the tool.
  • Blend edges (on, in green): where two pieces overlap the ink of both shows and one piece's paper does not hide the other's lines. Overlap the pieces slightly at the join and no cut or white strip is left; it also applies to the sheet sent to the editor. Each piece's outline only shows when selected or under the mouse.

The ruler and the protractor are aids: they do not appear on the editor sheet or in the DXF.

2. Sheet layers

  • Recognize: check which utilities the app should read (Electrical, Storm drain, Water, Sewer, Gas and Telecom). They all come checked; All checks or unchecks them at once. Only the ones the sheet has can be checked, and at least one is needed to continue. If you check a utility with all its layers hidden, nothing would be recognized: its checkbox shows a warning and Continue is disabled until you click Show its layers or Don't recognize it.
  • Plan layers: layers are grouped by utility (Water, Sewer, Storm drain, Gas, Electrical, Telecom, Other), each with its color. The utility's checkbox turns all its layers on or off; the ▸ arrow expands it so you can choose layer by layer. What you uncheck is hidden from the plan and not used for recognition.
  • Sheet without layers (flattened PDF): if the PDF has no layers —a map from a GIS system or a PDF «flattened» by Bluebeam, like the Phoenix Quarter Section maps—, its lines are grouped by style: color, weight and stroke type (for example Black 0.73 pt · continuous), and they all start in Others. Click a style to see its lines highlighted and, below the list, choose its Utility and Role (lines or structures): it moves to that utility's group and only the utilities you assigned a style to are recognized (until you assign one, Continue is disabled; Cancel leaves the sheet loaded to draw by hand). If that style's lines are continuous strokes, each stroke of the plan is taken as is as the pipe centerline (nothing joined or moved) and, in sewer and storm drain, a manhole is placed where two map pipes end. If the network is in an image (Phoenix's blue storm drain), there are no vectors to assign: draw it by hand. The style checkboxes only exclude them from recognition; the PDF still looks the same.
  • Scale: the Scale 1"=X' button at the bottom shows the sheet's scale, read from its text. If the text cannot be read or the PDF is printed reduced (the title block says 1"=100' and it is not), correct it before continuing: Calibrate with a known distance… (two clicks on the sheet and the real distance in feet; Esc exits) or Type the scale…. Curve radii and lengths are computed with it when recognizing.
  • By the letters on the line: the app does not only look at the layer name; it also reads the letters each line carries (—TE—, —SC—, —W—, —G—, —ss—…). A layer whose name is not a utility's, but whose lines say so, appears under its utility with the letters next to it, for example U-TRPW-DBNK-P (517) «TE» under Electrical (hover to see why), and it is recognized. The letters follow the plan's legend: «TE» and «SE» are electrification duct banks (Electrical), «SC» signal & communication (Telecom). In layers that mix everything (_Xref, G-XREF, layer 0) each line goes to the utility of its letters and the rest is left alone. Overhead «(oh)» and unknown «unk» lines are not recognized. A layer with lines of two utilities (G-XREF with telecom «—T—» lines and a water «—W—» segment) shows in each one's group, with its letter and a link icon: it is one single PDF layer, so hiding it hides it in both. To see only one utility's lines, check it in the Legend (they are highlighted with a halo).
  • «Legend» panel (on the left): at the top, By the letters on their line lists those layers as cards: the utility and its letters, the layer, why (its name does not say the utility, or says another one) and what the PDF's legend says about those letters. Each card's checkbox decides whether they are used: unchecked, the layer goes back to its group by name and is not recognized by its letters (the decision is saved in the project). Below, This sheet's legend, by utility (all of them): each kind of line on the sheet with a drawn sample —its color, dashed if existing and continuous if proposed, the letters read on its lines and «//» if it is to be abandoned— and what the PDF's own legend says about it (in electrical, for example: «e» EXISTING ELECTRICAL, «E» PROPOSED ELECTRICAL, «TE» and «SE» Metro's electrical ductbanks). If the PDF has no legend (DU06), the description comes from the BOE standard (the «BOE CADD Standards» manual: each utility's linetypes and the status in each layer name —E existing, D to demolish, A abandoned, N new—; with no status in the name, UPPERCASE = proposed). Overhead lines («e(oh)») have their own row with «Not recognized»: they show with their utility in «Plan layers», but they are not imported.
  • See it on the sheet (checkboxes): a utility's checkbox checks all its lines and structures (upper or lower case: what will be recognized); a row's, only that kind of line; clicking the utility or the row does the same. You can check several: everything checked is highlighted together on the sheet, each utility in its color, and in the panel it gets a border and background of that color (the utility's checkbox shows partial if only some of its kinds are checked). A letters card adds with a click: its lines with a halo and, as a thin line, the rest of its utility. The top of the panel says what is highlighted and how many layers; another click removes that one, Show all unchecks everything. Hover a row to see its sample large (and the PDF legend's, if it has one). Below, collapsed, the Full PDF legend (this sheet's rows in bold; click = their lines). If the sheet has no utilities and no layers by letters, the panel collapses by itself (‹ / › collapses and expands it).
  • Type in Search layer… to find a layer by name; the eye buttons show or hide all the layers visible in the list.
  • Opacity lightens the plan (and switches the background to white or black), just like in the editor.
  • If the sheet has several pages, ◀ Sheet N / M ▶ changes sheet without leaving.
  • To go back to step 1, click 1 Compose sheet in the bar at the top; Continue recognizes the sheet and opens step 3. If you cancel here, the sheet is loaded in the editor without recognition, so you can draw by hand.

3. Recognition preview

  • Shows the lines found for each utility, each with its color, and their vaults or manholes. The screen has three columns: To check on the left, the sheet in the middle and the summary on the right.
  • On the right: the sheet and its scale, cards with the totals (segments, abandoned, bends, structures), a bar per utility (active solid, abandoned hatched) and the Coverage: how much of the plan's strokes was covered (green ✔ = complete). If the quality check marked something on the sheet, it says what below: uncovered dashes (magenta line), vaults without a line (magenta ring) and off-pattern strokes (dotted turquoise: arrows, leaders). Those colors belong to no utility.
  • At the bottom, Layers used: the layers taken as lines and vaults. Click one (or pick it with the arrow keys and press Enter) and the sheet shows it: that layer's items get a halo in their color, the rest fades and the view zooms to that area; below the title it says how many lines and vaults there are. Clicking Lines, Vaults or the utility name shows that whole group. Another click on the same row, or ✕ Show all, shows the whole sheet again.
  • On the left, To check: these are not errors. The sheet was already recognized; these are points worth a look before importing (a curve imported as an approximation, a vault no line reaches…). Each one says below what was found and what was done with it. Only what shows in red is a real problem (for example, a sheet without layers). The points are grouped by utility. Click one (or reach it with Tab and press Enter): the view takes you to the exact spot on the sheet, marked with a black and white box. Each click shows the next case (1/N). Once you have seen all its cases it is marked ✔ and every visited spot on the sheet keeps a dashed green box; at the top you see your progress («1 of 4 seen»). One with no spot is marked with one click; right-click sets it back to pending. Extra information is folded under Details. The ‹ button collapses the panel to see the sheet larger; collapsed, a strip shows «To check (N)».
  • At the bottom left: Opacity lightens the plan so you can better see what and how much was recognized, and Join segments (green = on) joins segments that continue straight ahead into one pipe.
  • To go back, click 2 Sheet layers or 1 Compose sheet in the bar at the top. Adjust layers… is only needed if the plan uses other layer names: there you say which are lines and which are vaults (also if you disagree with a line that was recognized by its letters: what you choose there rules).
  • Continue and import into the editor adds them to the project as pipes; Cancel leaves the sheet in the editor, without lines (if you already had lines in the editor and came back to compose, it leaves it as it was).
  • Abandoned lines are imported marked (AB): those on the «-A» layer with «/» marks, and any line with double «//» marks.
  • Curved bends on the plan are imported with their real radius, including very open curves, small rounded corners, a curve that leaves another line or ends loose and two curves joined with no straight segment between them (an «S» curve: one bend per curve, touching at the inflection point). Each bend covers its whole curve on the plan, end to end. A dashed bend is approximate (on the plan it is not an exact arc): check it.
  • In electrical, telecommunications, storm drain and sewer every kink of the line (where it changes direction with no vault or branch) is imported as a curve: there are no kinks in the field (also the kinks of a line that the plan draws as a single stroke of several straight pieces). It gets the minimum radius of the rule (automatic: 6 times the pipe's inside width; if you change the diameter, the curve follows it), so the drawing error does not grow. Where the minimum curve does not fit (very short segments, or another curve would be trimmed) the kink stays. Water and gas do not change: in a pressure network a kink is a fitting bend.
  • Each line ends where its ink ends: if on the plan it stops in mid-air it is not joined to the neighboring line. If it stops at its letter (or at a gap) right before the edge of the view, it reaches the plan's cut; on a composite sheet, lines that cross the seam still run from one piece to the other. A line whose letter is covered by a text label on the plan stays continuous.
  • Magnet to vaults and manholes: a line that reaches a vault or manhole and was left less than 3 pt from its edge is brought to the drawn edge, along its own line (no kinks); a loose line, only if it is less than 1.5 pt away. On import, the box, solid or manhole is joined to that line instead of left loose at the center. A vault with no line nearby is not touched. The Line ends joined to their vault notice takes you to each one.
  • If a pipe is drawn with its two walls (two parallel lines running together from start to end) a single utility is imported, along the middle line. The same if it has walls and a centerline (the centerline is used), walls and a colored filled body, dashed walls or a thin closed outline (its centerline is used). It applies to every utility. The notice pipes with two walls takes you to each one. Three or more parallel lines together are left untouched.
  • If the plan has the same line drawn twice on the same layer (overlapping, with shifted dashes), a single line is imported.
  • Sewer and storm drain come in with their manholes; electrical and telecom, with a box only where the plan has a vault. Water and gas are pressure networks: their vertices get no nodes, but their vaults do come in.
  • Every vault you see in the preview reaches the editor, also the ones with no line at all (magenta ring): they come in as a standalone box with their shape and size (rectangular ones as SÓLIDO), without touching the lines. This also applies to pull boxes, poles and proposed structures.

Composing again later

Menu Tools → Compose work sheet… opens step 1 again, on the PDF and sheet you were working on, and repeats steps 2 and 3 with the new sheet. Everything is saved in the project (.digproj), including the source PDFs.

If you continue without changing anything (not the composition, the layers or the utilities), the preview opens instantly with the previous recognition: it stays in memory while the project is open. Any change in those steps is enough for the sheet to be recognized again.

What you already have in the editor is not lost until you import: Cancel in any of the three steps (or an error while recognizing) leaves the editor exactly as it was, with the same sheet, its lines, Undo and unsaved changes. Only Continue and import into the editor replaces what was there.

Tip: if something is not recognized, you can always draw it by hand (next chapter).
  1. Open the Draw utility section of the left panel.
  2. Choose the type: water, sewer, storm drain, gas, electrical or telecom.
  3. Click on the plan at each point the pipe passes through.
  4. Press  Enter  to finish.

The green magnet (to join pipes precisely)

When you get close to another pipe of the same type, a green mark appears. Its shape tells you what the click will snap to:

Circle: the end of another pipe.
Square: a vertex (a bend) of another pipe.
Triangle: a point in the middle of a segment.
Diamond: the edge or a corner of a solid (of any utility).

The same magnet works when you edit/move: dragging the first or last vertex of a pipe snaps it to another pipe of the same type or to the edge of a solid.

If you start at the end of another pipe of the same type, the app asks whether to continue it (same utility) or create a new one that touches it.

Tip: to fix a pipe, select it in the list and use Edit/move (right click), or Precise move to move it by an exact distance.

Joins two or more utilities of the same type into a single one (one polyline and one network in Civil 3D). It is useful, for example, when recognition left a pipe split into several pieces.

How to do it

  1. Select the utilities, in one of these two ways:
    • In the Utilities list:  Ctrl  + click on each one.
    • On the plan:  Ctrl  + click on each line. Selected ones look thicker; another Ctrl + click on one removes it.
  2. Press  Ctrl+J , or right click (in the list or on one of the lines on the plan) → Join into one utility. It is also in Edit → Join selected utilities.
  3. A preview appears: the resulting utility as a dashed green line (with its curves, as on the canvas) and a green circle at each joint. Read the message and press Yes to join or No to leave it as it was.

What must be true

End to end They are joined at their ends. If they touch (less than 0.5 ft apart) they are joined at one vertex. If there is a gap of up to 10 ft, it is closed with a straight run and the message warns you first.
Same type Water with water, gas with gas… Types are not mixed.
Branches (tees) If a selected one starts in the middle of another selected one, the through one is cut at the tee: the join follows the selected ones and the piece that does not fit stays as a separate utility (in amber in the preview). Utilities you did not select are never touched. If the selected ones branch so much that they do not fit in a single line, the app explains it: join them in parts.

What happens to the data

  • The first utility you selected wins: its name, diameter, material and family go to the whole joined utility. If any had other values, the preview message tells you which change.
  • The elevations of each run are kept; at each joint the arriving and leaving elevations stay.
  • Curves, manholes and boxes, the duct bank and approved vertical connections are kept.
  • If one was drawn backwards, it is reversed automatically.
Tip: everything is undone at once with  Ctrl+Z .

Set the family, the diameter (size), the pipe type, the material or the status on many utilities at once, or copy them from one utility to others. Only between utilities of the same type (electrical with electrical, water with water…): families depend on the type.

Bulk edit

  1. Select the utilities:  Ctrl  + click in the list or on the plan, or right-click → Select all of type …
  2. Press  Ctrl+E , or right-click → Bulk edit.
  3. Every field starts as (no change): choose only what you want to change and press Apply.
  • The size is chosen after the family. If the selected utilities have different families, choose the family first.
  • Those with a duct bank keep their family and size (the duct bank defines them); the other fields do change.

Copy properties

  1. Right-click the utility that is already right → Copy properties ( Ctrl+Shift+C ).
  2. Select the others and right-click → Paste properties ( Ctrl+Shift+V ). The same window opens with the copied values: change them or leave as (no change) what you don't want to paste. Utilities of another type are left untouched.

With several selected there is also Copy properties of #N to the others: the first one you selected passes its data to the rest.

Tip: everything is undone at once with  Ctrl+Z .

Adding a size that is not in the catalog

The green + button to the right of Size (in Utilities and in Manholes) adds a new size to the chosen family:

  1. Choose the family first.
  2. Press + and type the size: the diameter, or the width and height (or length, for manholes).
  3. The wall thickness and other data are calculated from the neighboring sizes; you can correct them.
  4. Check which Civil 3D gets it (every version from 2025 on, in Spanish and English) and press Add size. The new size is selected.

When the DXF is imported, the plugin updates the Civil 3D catalog and draws the exact size.

Water and gas: close Civil 3D before adding the size (its catalog is in use while it is open). The app also creates the bends and other fittings of that diameter.
If you see «No permission to write the catalog», run the app as administrator.

Extended data

The green View extended data button (next to «Delete», also in Manholes) shows a Field | Value table:

  • If the pipe or vault came from the PDF, it carries locked 🔒 data: the layer it came from and what its name says (type, whether it is new, existing or abandoned, and the source sheet). They are for reference only.
  • With Add field you write down your own data. It is saved in the project and goes into the DXF.
  • In Civil 3D you see them when you select the part: Properties palette → Extended Data tab. Each pipe (and fitting) carries «PDFCAD_Utilidad» (utility type, its number in the app list, the network and this data) and each manhole or box «PDFCAD_Estructura» (its code, the network and its data). Your own fields show as «Usuario_…».
Important about the name: the name only identifies the network. Two pipes of the same type that touch are joined in Civil 3D even if their names differ: they go into the network of the first name (a name you typed wins over a default one). The ones that do not touch go into separate networks.

Curves

In the Curves list you can turn a bend into a curve and set its radius (in feet). If the number turns red, the radius does not fit the neighboring segments: the app shows the maximum allowed. Curves that came from a kink in the plan show Source: kink in the plan; «Treat as manhole/box again» removes them.

  • The app automatically places a manhole (BZ-) at each sewer and storm drain vertex.
  • Electrical and telecom networks have few boxes: the app only places a box (CAJA-) where the PDF plan has a real vault, with its measurements. You add the rest.
  • In the Manholes list you change their family, size and elevations. If a size is missing, the green + button adds it (see «Pipe data»).
  • Square boxes coming from the PDF show as SÓLIDO (solid): no family; you adjust their Length and Width (the drawing resizes) and their Height (2 m by default). The Top elevation drives the top: by default it is calculated so the attached utility reaches the middle of the solid (its centerline at mid-height; ↺ returns to it). In Civil 3D they are created as 3D solids with their extended data as a Property Set.
  • To add one in the middle of a line: Tools → Insert manhole in-line…
  • Hide manhole removes a manhole that does not really exist; the network stays connected.
Water and gas are pressure networks: they have no manholes. Where pipes meet, Civil 3D places a fitting (elbow, Tee, Wye or cross). Their vaults recognized from the PDF come in as SÓLIDO.

A duct bank is a concrete block with several conduits inside.

  1. Right-click an electrical or telecom pipe → Create duct bank for this pipe (or the Open Duct Bank Designer button).
  2. Draw the section: block size, margins and rounded corners.
  3. Add the conduits. The outline is green if it fits and red if it breaks a rule (minimum spacing or edge clearance).
  4. Save and close. Civil 3D creates the 3D block with its conduits.

One design for several pipes. In Assign to: check every pipe that carries the same duct bank (the filter and the All / None buttons help). Hover a pipe in the list to see its thumbnail on the plan before checking it. You can also select several in the Utilities list ( Ctrl +click,  Shift +click or  Ctrl+A ) and right-click → Create one duct bank for the N utilities or Assign existing duct bank. In the Duct banks list, hover a row to see the section.

Tip: the Show dimensions button draws the distances between conduits.  Esc  drops the active tool without closing the window.

While you draw, the app checks the design and places signs where something needs your attention. Hover over a sign to read what is going on, or click it for the details. They only appear at crossings and joints of pressure network pipes (water and gas). On storm drain, sewer, electrical and telecom you are only warned when two pipes cross at the same elevation without being connected («Clashing pipes»).

Fittings on the plan

In water and gas, next to each point where Civil 3D will place a solid fitting there is a label with its type and angle: «Bend 45°», «Tee 90°», «Wye 45°», «Cross 90°».

For a bend, the angle is measured on the axis: the axis of the straight side is extended and you read how much the other side deviates (0° = straight). A commercial «45°» bend reads 45°, as it is purchased. The same angle goes to the bend's Property Set in Civil 3D.

Dark label Meets the active design standards.
Red label ✗ Breaks a mandatory standard. Hover to see which one and the closest allowed angle.
Amber label ✗ Breaks a recommended standard.

The status bar shows «✗ N outside the standards»; click it to open the window. To hide the labels: View → Show fittings (type and angle) (checking keeps working). The app only warns: it does not move anything in the drawing.

The standards window

  1. On the left, pick a category (fittings, clearances, cover, other requirements) or Violations, each with a Show on the plan button.
  2. Each rule is one row: the switch (active in this project), its name, the value (for example «≥ 5 ft») and its status (✓ meets, ✗ fails, or «Not checked on the plan yet»).
  3. Details opens everything else: original text, measured from, references, notes, Mandatory / Recommended (red or amber warning) and the values to edit them.
  4. Search rule… filters by any word («gas track», «cover»…).
  5. A− / A+ change the text size. Everything works with the keyboard: Tab to move, Space or Enter to activate.

Initial values (AWWA): bends of 11.25°, 22.5°, 45° and 90° (deviation on the axis); tee at 90°; wye at 45°; cross at 90°; tolerance ±1°. Clearances, cover and requirements that come from the Excel are stored, edited and exported, but they are not checked on the plan yet (each row says so).

Simple format (the official one for new standards)

Export Excel saves the format proposed by the civil engineers, in English and readable without the app. Import Excel recognizes it automatically (also the template below and the clearance tables).

  • All (Flat): one row per rule — Utility A, Utility B (another utility, RAILROAD TRACK, CURB & GUTTER, CATCH BASIN, MAINTENANCE HOLE or COVER = depth of cover), Orientation (Horizontal/Vertical), Case («General» or the condition), Min and Max with their unit (ft or in), Measured From, Notes, Reference(s) («W-2:7») and Source Sheet. A row without a number is a written requirement.
  • References: Source Sheet, Ref # and the text of each reference.
  • One tab per utility (a copy for reading; edit «All (Flat)»), Legend and How to Add a Utility. The grey columns on the right (Rule ID, Active, Mandatory, Status, Note IDs) are filled by the app; leave them empty in new rules.

Whatever the app does not know (a new element, a reference missing from «References») is still imported with the Review status.

Standards in Excel (for the engineers)

Previously, rules were exchanged with a universal Excel template (it can still be imported): one row per rule, with drop-down lists, so the engineer does not have to type free text.

Export Excel Saves the template with all the rules you have now (changed or not), with their references and notes. It also works as a blank format: add rows at the end.
Import Excel Reads the filled template. Before saving it shows how many rules are new, how many are updated and the rows with errors (with their row number); those are not imported. It also accepts the clearance tables Excel the engineers used: it converts it automatically.

Template sheets:

  • Instrucciones: how to fill it in, with an example and the meaning of each column.
  • Reglas: Type, Utility, Condition, Against (another utility or object: railroad track, curb, manhole…), Minimum and Maximum in feet, Measured from, References, Notes, Mandatory, Active and Status. The grey columns are the traceability: the text as it came in the source table.
  • Referencias (which book, standard or email each rule comes from), Notas (clarifications) and Documento (source file, titles and tables).
Nothing is lost: when the tables Excel is converted, every text ends up in a rule, a reference, a note or the Documento sheet. Whatever the conversion did not fully understand (conditions, exceptions) gets the Revisar (review) status so the engineer can fix it in the template.

Review and clean up the drawing

When exporting, if something would come out wrong in Civil 3D, the app shows it first and fixes it with Fix and export (or goes on as it is with Export without changes). It is also in Tools → Review and clean up the drawing… Click each case to see it on the plan; uncheck what you don't want fixed.

  • Tiny straight runs (under 1 ft): they came out as a tiny pipe in Civil 3D. The extra vertex is removed, or the neighboring curve starts exactly at the vertex (a slightly larger radius).
  • Ends that almost touch (up to 1 ft): the end is joined exactly to the other utility of the same type. Never sideways against a parallel line.
  • Utilities shorter than 1 ft or repeated (the same line twice): they are deleted.
  • To review by hand: what is not fixed automatically (for example next to a manhole) and short utilities that touch no other of their type.

In Civil 3D

  1. Open the plugin panel (command PANEL_REDES).
  2. Press Import network from DXF and choose the file.
  3. The networks are created with their pipes, manholes, curves and duct banks.

What you will see in 3D

  • On water and gas, joints get solid fittings: elbow (bend), Tee (90° branch), Wye (angled branch) and cross (4 pipes). Pipes go into the fitting's bell.
  • Each fitting stores its data (type, angle, diameters, material, network) in its properties.
  • Approved vertical joints are drawn with elbows and a vertical segment.
  • Each pipe, manhole or box and fitting goes on its utility's layer (ELECTRICO, TELECOM, AGUA…), the same as the DXF lines: you can turn each utility on or off by its layer. The DXF 2D lines are erased once they become the network. The conduits of a duct bank go on the duct bank's layer. Solids too: water and gas fittings drawn as a 3D solid (bend, Tee, Wye, reducer) go on their network's layer, and the solids that represent manholes or boxes on their utility's layer.
Attention: if you use your own families, install them first with Tools → Install custom family… and then run PREPARAR_FAMILIAS in Civil 3D.

A summary to explain the process to a client or someone who does not use the app. The information goes through three stages:

1 · The app
You draw or recognize the pipes on the plan and fill in their data.		 ➜ 2 · The DXF file
It carries the lines and, attached to each one, a «data card».		 ➜ 3 · The Civil 3D plugin
It reads each card and builds the network in 3D.

1. In the app: the drawing and its data

Each utility is a line with points on the plan. The app adds to that line what Civil 3D needs to know and the paper plan does not say by itself:

  • What it is: water, gas, storm drain, sewer, electrical or telecom.
  • What it is like: diameter, material, Civil 3D catalog family and size.
  • How deep it goes: elevations at the start, at the end and at each bend.
  • What it carries: manholes or boxes (with their rim and sump), curves with their radius, solids, duct banks and whether the pipe is abandoned.
  • Where it is in the world: the georeference (real Los Angeles coordinates).

Everything in feet. The plan scale turns each drawn point into its real measurement.

2. The DXF file: lines + hidden cards

When you press Export DXF a standard drawing file is created:

One line per pipe With its vertices in real coordinates, on one layer per utility type.
One point per manhole or box At its location, with its code, elevations and dimensions.
A «card» on each object Data stored inside the object itself (diameter, material, elevations, network…). It is not visible in the drawing but travels with it. Only the plugin knows how to read it.

If you open that DXF in AutoCAD you will only see lines and points: the cards travel inside them without being drawn.

3. In Civil 3D: the plugin builds the network

With Import network from DXF the plugin goes through the file and reads each object's card:

  1. It groups the pipes into networks: pipes of the same type that touch end up in the same network.
  2. It picks the parts from the Civil 3D catalog for each diameter and material.
  3. It raises everything to its height with the card's elevations. If there is a ground surface, it uses it for the manhole rims.
  4. It joins the parts according to the network type:
    • Water and gas: solid bends, tees, wyes and crosses at each joint.
    • Storm drain and sewer: manholes at the vertices.
    • Electrical and telecom: boxes only where there are any, and curves with their radius.
  5. It adds the extras: 3D solids for the boxes, duct banks that follow the pipe, the dashed style of abandoned pipes and each object's extra data (Property Sets).
  6. It applies the project's coordinate system so everything sits in its real place.

The result is native Civil 3D objects (pipe networks, structures, fittings), not loose drawings: they can be edited, labeled, shown in profile and measured like any network built by hand in Civil 3D.

In one sentence: the app draws and adds the data, the DXF carries it hidden inside each line, and the plugin reads it to create the real network in 3D.

To add extra information to the pipes in Civil 3D (installation date, specification…):

  1. In the plugin panel: Export pipes to Excel. A sheet with each pipe's name and type is created.
  2. In Excel, add one column per piece of data and fill it in. Leave blank what doesn't apply.
  3. In the panel: Import Property Sets from Excel.
  4. Check it in Civil 3D: select a pipe → Properties → Extended Data.

You can import the same file several times: nothing gets duplicated.

Fitting data (elbows, tees, wyes, crosses)

Every fitting the plugin creates carries its data automatically. Select it → Properties → Extended Data → PDFCAD_Accesorio:

  • Tipo_Accesorio: fitting type (Codo = elbow, Tee, Wye, Cruz = cross).
  • Angulo_Grados: the fitting angle, in degrees.
  • Diametro_Pulg: the diameter in inches («12 x 8» if one outlet is smaller).
  • Material: the pipe material.
  • Cota_Eje_Pies: the elevation of the fitting's center, in feet.

I don't see my pipes in Civil 3D

Check that you chose the same Civil 3D version and language on the top bar, and that the pipes have elevations.

A yellow sign appears where two pipes join

That is normal: hover over it to see which fitting Civil 3D will place. If it says they clash, fix the elevation.

I renamed a pipe and now it is in another network

Pipes of the same type that touch always go into the same network; it takes the first name.

I made a mistake

 Ctrl+Z  undoes it. If you closed without saving on purpose («Don't save»), open the last saved .digproj.

The app closed suddenly (power cut, crash…)

While you work, the app saves a backup of your changes every 2 minutes by itself (only when there are unsaved changes). If it closes without you closing it, the next time you open it you are offered to Recover that backup. The project opens as «unsaved»: press  Ctrl+S  to save it to its usual file. You can also Discard the backup or decide Later.

The backup is deleted by itself when you save, when you choose not to save and when you close the app normally. It lives in %LOCALAPPDATA%\pdf-to-cad ecuperacion; it does not replace saving the project.
